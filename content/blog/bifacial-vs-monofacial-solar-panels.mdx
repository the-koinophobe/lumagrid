Some new panels can catch sunlight on **both sides**. They are called **bifacial**. Normal panels catch light on one side. They are called **monofacial**. Should you pay for two sides?

## How the back side works

Sunlight hits the ground or roof and bounces up. A bifacial panel can catch some of that bounced light on its back. That makes a little extra power.

But this only works if light can actually reach the back.

## Side by side

<div className="table-scroll">

| | Bifacial | Monofacial |
|---|---|---|
| Sides that make power | Two | One |
| Extra power on a roof | Very little | None |
| Extra power on raised ground mount | About 5 to 15% | None |
| Weight | Often heavier (glass-glass) | Lighter |
| Price | Higher | Lower |

</div>

## Roofs in Port Harcourt and Warri

Most homes put panels on rails close to the roof sheet. The gap is small and dark. Little light reaches the back. So a bifacial panel acts almost like a normal one. You pay more for little gain.

## Where bifacial makes sense

- **Ground mounts** in a compound with space, raised high
- **Carports** over a light-coloured floor
- **Flat concrete roofs** painted white, with panels raised on frames
- **Schools, churches and farms** with open land

Read [roof mount vs ground mount](/blog/roof-mount-vs-ground-mount-solar) to see which suits you.

## A note on weight

Many bifacial panels have glass on both sides. They can be heavier. On older roofs in places like [Sapele](/solar-installation/sapele), we check the roof first.

## Our advice

For a normal home roof: buy a good, genuine monofacial mono panel. For an open ground site: ask us about bifacial.
