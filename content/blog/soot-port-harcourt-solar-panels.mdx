If you live in **Port Harcourt**, you know the black soot. It is on cars, floors and clothes. It also lands on solar panels.

## How soot hurts output

Panels need light to reach the cells. Soot blocks it. The film is oily, so it sticks.

<div className="table-scroll">

| Panel condition | Output loss (rough) |
|---|---|
| Clean | 0% |
| Light dust | 2–5% |
| Visible soot film | 5–15% |
| Thick dirt and droppings | More |

</div>

## How often to clean

- **Dry season / harmattan:** every 4 to 6 weeks
- **Rainy season:** after long dry spells
- **Any time** you see a dark film

## Safe cleaning steps

1. Clean early morning or late afternoon, when panels are cool.
2. Use **clean water** and a **soft brush** on a long pole.
3. A little mild soap if needed. Rinse well.
4. No abrasive powders. No pressure washers.
5. Do not stand on the panels.

On an [upstairs building](/blog/solar-on-upstairs-building), leave roof work to people with proper access gear. Read [how to clean solar panels safely](/blog/how-to-clean-solar-panels-safely).

## Check your gain

Look at your inverter's daily solar output before and after cleaning. You will see the difference.
