"Is it safe to keep a lithium battery inside my house?" We hear this in almost every home in Port Harcourt. The short answer is **yes**, if it is the right type and installed properly.

## Why LiFePO4 is the safe choice

There are many lithium types. Home solar uses **LiFePO4**. It is very stable. It does not overheat easily. Read [LiFePO4 vs NMC](/blog/lifepo4-vs-nmc-lithium-battery).

## Lithium vs lead-acid safety

<div className="table-scroll">

| | LiFePO4 lithium | Lead-acid (tubular, gel) |
|---|---|---|
| Acid that can spill | No | Tubular: yes |
| Gas while charging | No | Yes (hydrogen) |
| Built-in protection | BMS | None |
| Heavy to move | Lighter | Very heavy |

</div>

## The four safety rules

### 1. Good battery with a BMS

The BMS stops overcharge, over-drain and shorts. See [what a BMS is](/blog/what-is-a-bms-lithium-battery).

### 2. Right cables and fuses

Thick enough cables. A DC fuse or breaker between battery and inverter. This is the most important part of the install.

### 3. Correct inverter settings

The inverter must charge at lithium voltages. Wrong settings stress the battery.

### 4. A good place

- Dry and cool
- Air can move
- No fuel, paint or clothes nearby
- Out of children's reach
- Above flood level in places like **Yenagoa** and **Sagbama**

## Lightning and storms

Rainy-season storms bring lightning. Good earthing and surge protection protect the inverter and battery. See [earthing and surge protection](/blog/earthing-and-surge-protection-for-solar).
