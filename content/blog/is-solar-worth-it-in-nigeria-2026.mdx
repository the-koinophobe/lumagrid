Solar is not cheap, and anyone who tells you it is "free power" is selling something. So here is the honest version.

## The short answer

If you run a generator most days, **yes, solar is worth it in 2026**, and it gets more worth it every time fuel goes up.

## The numbers

At about ₦1,250 per litre of petrol in Port Harcourt (August 2026):

<div className="table-scroll">

| Generator use | Fuel per day | Fuel per month | Fuel per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small gen, 5 hrs/day | ~4 litres | ~₦150,000 | ~₦1.8M |
| Small gen, 8–10 hrs/day | ~8 litres | ~₦300,000 | ~₦3.6M |
| Bigger gen for AC, 10 hrs/day | ~15 litres | ~₦560,000 | ~₦6.8M |

</div>

Add roughly ₦180,000 a year for servicing, plugs, oil and repairs, and a replacement generator every few years.

Now compare that with a one-off solar system:

- **3kVA** with 3.2kWh lithium: from about ₦1.7M
- **6kVA** with 8kWh lithium (runs one AC): from about ₦3.8M

A household spending ₦300,000 a month on fuel pays back a ₦3.8M system in about 13 months. After that, the money stays in your pocket for the 8 to 15 years the battery lasts and the 25 years the panels last. See the full [five-year generator vs solar comparison](/blog/generator-vs-solar), or put in your own fuel spend in the [solar calculator](/calculator).

## Who gains the most

- **Homes running a generator every evening** in [Port Harcourt](/solar-installation/port-harcourt), [Warri](/solar-installation/warri), [Yenagoa](/solar-installation/yenagoa) and anywhere grid supply is weak
- **Shops and small businesses** that lose sales or stock when power goes. See [solar for small businesses](/blog/solar-for-small-businesses-in-rivers-state)
- **Island and riverine communities** like [Bonny](/solar-installation/bonny) and [Brass](/solar-installation/brass), where fuel costs more
- **Landlords** of lodges and flats, where one system ends daily fuel arguments

## When solar is less worth it

- You get **near-24-hour stable grid supply**. Solar then mainly cuts your bill, and payback is slower. Read [what solar replaces on your PHCN bill](/blog/phcn-bill).
- You **rarely use a generator** and can live with outages.
- You can only afford a system **too small for your loads**. An undersized system disappoints and its batteries die early. It is better to start with a correctly sized small system and add to it.
