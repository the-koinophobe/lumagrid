Many people in Port Harcourt start with tubular or gel batteries. They are cheaper in the shop. But after one or two years, the batteries die. Then the person buys new ones. Then they die again.

Lithium-ion batteries stop that cycle. This guide explains why, in simple words.

## What is a lithium-ion battery?

It is the same family of battery that is in your phone. For solar, we use a special kind called **LiFePO4**. You say it "life-po-four". It means lithium iron phosphate.

LiFePO4 is the safest lithium type. It does not catch fire easily. It also lasts the longest.

## Reason 1: It lasts much longer

Every night, your battery gives power. Every day, the sun fills it again. One empty-and-fill is called a **cycle**.

<div className="table-scroll">

| Battery | Cycles | Years with daily use |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium (LiFePO4) | 3,000 to 6,000 | 8 to 15 years |
| Gel | 500 to 800 | 2 to 3 years |
| Tubular | 400 to 600 | 1.5 to 2.5 years |

</div>

So one lithium battery can outlast **four to six** tubular batteries.

## Reason 2: You can use more of it

A tubular battery should only be used halfway down. If you go lower, it gets damaged fast.

A lithium battery can go down to about 10 or 20 percent. So:

- A **5kWh lithium** battery gives you about **4 to 4.5kWh** to use.
- A **5kWh tubular** bank gives you only about **2.5kWh** to use.

That means lithium gives you almost **double** the night power from the same size label.

## Reason 3: It charges faster

In the rainy season, the sun in Port Harcourt, Warri and Yenagoa comes and goes. You may only get a few good hours.

Lithium takes in charge quickly and wastes very little. About 95 out of every 100 units of solar power goes into the battery. Tubular wastes more, often 15 to 30 units in every 100.

So on a cloudy day, lithium fills up better.

## Reason 4: No care needed

Tubular batteries need distilled water. They give off gas. They can leak acid on your floor.

Lithium needs none of that. You put it in a dry, cool room and leave it.

## Reason 5: Small and light

A 200Ah tubular battery weighs about 60 to 70kg. A lithium battery with similar usable power can weigh less than half of that. Many lithium batteries hang on the wall. That is great for flats and upstairs buildings with small rooms.

## Reason 6: It protects itself

Every good lithium battery has a **BMS**. That means battery management system. It is a small brain inside. It stops the battery from:

- Charging too much
- Draining too low
- Getting too hot
- Short circuits

Read more in [what a BMS is](/blog/what-is-a-bms-lithium-battery).

## But lithium costs more. Is it worth it?

Let us do simple maths for **10 years**:

<div className="table-scroll">

| | Tubular | Lithium |
|---|---|---|
| How many times you buy | About 5 times | Once |
| Usable power each night | Half the label | Most of the label |
| Water and care | Yes | No |
| Cost per year | Higher | Lower |

</div>

Lithium storage costs roughly ₦220,000 per kWh in our installed systems. It sounds like a lot. But you buy it once, not five times.

## When to be careful

Not all lithium batteries are good. Some are fake or use weak cells. Before you buy:

1. Ask for the brand and the cell type.
2. Check that it is **LiFePO4**.
3. Ask about the BMS.
4. Get a warranty of at least 5 years in writing.
