Bayelsa knows floods. The 2012 and 2022 floods put large parts of Yenagoa, Sagbama, Southern Ijaw and Kolokuma/Opokuma under water, and many low-lying streets flood in heavy rainy seasons even without a major event. Parts of Rivers and Delta along the rivers face the same risk.

That does not make solar a bad idea there. It just changes **where** things go.

## What a flood actually damages

<div className="table-scroll">

| Part | Usual location | Flood risk |
|---|---|---|
| Solar panels | Roof | Very low |
| Cables from roof | Wall, conduit | Low, if entries are sealed |
| Inverter | Indoor wall | **High** if at ground level |
| Batteries | Floor of a room | **Very high** if on the floor |
| Distribution board | Indoor wall | Medium |

</div>

Batteries placed on the floor are the most common casualty. Water and electronics do not mix, and a submerged battery can be a fire hazard.

## How we install in flood-prone areas

### 1. Height first

We ask where water reached in past floods, then mount the inverter and batteries well above that line. On an [upstairs building](/blog/solar-on-upstairs-building), the equipment goes on the upper floor. On a bungalow, we use strong wall brackets or a raised platform fixed to the wall.

### 2. Lithium, not lead-acid

[LiFePO4 batteries](/blog/lifep04-vs-acid) are compact and wall-mountable, which makes height easier. Flooded lead-acid batteries are heavy, sit on the floor and contain acid that should never mix with flood water.

### 3. One clear isolator

A labelled isolator switch and battery breaker in an easy-to-reach, high position so anyone in the house can shut the system down fast.

### 4. Sealed cable entries

Every cable entering the building is in conduit, with sealed entry points, so water cannot track along the cable into the board.

## What to do before, during and after a flood

### Before (when warnings start)

- Check the battery level is high so you have power if the grid goes off
- Clear the battery room of anything that could float into the equipment
- Make sure everyone knows where the isolator is

### During (water rising toward the equipment)

- Switch the inverter off, open the DC and AC isolators and the battery breaker
- Keep people, especially children, away from the equipment
- Do not wade near any electrical equipment

### After

- Do **not** switch anything back on if water touched the inverter, battery or board
- Call a technician to inspect, dry and test it
- Rinse mud off the panels once the roof is safe to access

## Already have solar at ground level?

It can usually be moved. Relocating an inverter and battery higher up is a small job compared with replacing them after a flood.
