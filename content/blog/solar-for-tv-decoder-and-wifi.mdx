Football, news, Netflix, school work on Wi-Fi. These are small loads. Solar handles them easily.

## How much power

<div className="table-scroll">

| Item | Watts |
|---|---|
| 32-inch LED TV | 30–50W |
| 43-inch LED TV | 60–100W |
| 55-inch LED TV | 100–150W |
| Decoder (DStv, GOtv, etc.) | 15–25W |
| Wi-Fi router | About 10W |
| Sound bar | 20–60W |
| Phone charging | 5–20W each |

</div>

## Night example

TV (80W) + decoder (20W) + router (10W) + 2 phones (20W) = **130W**.

6 hours = **0.78kWh**.

A 3.2kWh lithium battery gives about 2.9kWh. That leaves plenty for lights and fans.

## Why solar is better than a generator for electronics

Generators can surge and drop. That stresses TVs and decoders. A pure sine wave inverter gives steady power.
