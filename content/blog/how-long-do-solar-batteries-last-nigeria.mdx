There are two questions hiding in "how long does a solar battery last?":

1. **How many years** before it needs replacing?
2. **How many hours** does it power your home each night?

We answer both.

## How many years: battery lifespan in Nigeria

Batteries wear out by cycles: one full drain and recharge. A home solar battery in Port Harcourt cycles almost every day, so cycle life translates directly into years.

<div className="table-scroll">

| Battery type | Usable capacity | Typical cycle life | Real lifespan with daily use |
|---|---|---|---|
| LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) | 80–95% | 3,000–6,000 | 8–15 years |
| Gel / AGM | ~50% | 500–800 | 2–3 years |
| Tubular (flooded lead-acid) | ~50% | 400–600 | 1.5–2.5 years |

</div>

That is why a lithium battery that costs more on day one usually costs less per year of use. We break down the maths in [LiFePO4 vs lead-acid](/blog/lifep04-vs-acid).

## What shortens battery life in Nigerian homes

- **Heat.** A battery in a hot, unventilated store room ages faster. Keep it in the coolest room you have, off the floor and out of direct sun.
- **Draining it flat.** Lead-acid batteries hate being discharged below 50%. Lithium handles deep discharge far better, but its BMS should still stop it before it hits zero.
- **Never fully charging.** This is the big one. If your system relies on a few hours of grid supply to charge (an "inverter-only" setup), the battery is rarely full. Lead-acid batteries sulphate and die within months. Read [inverter only vs full solar](/blog/inverter-only-vs-full-solar-system).
- **Too few panels.** In rainy season, an undersized panel array cannot refill the battery, so it sits half-empty for weeks.
- **Mixing old and new batteries.** The weakest battery drags the whole bank down.

## How many hours: backup time each night

Use this simple formula:

```
Backup hours = Usable battery kWh ÷ Load in kW
```

A 5kWh lithium battery gives about 4.5kWh usable.

- 300W load (lights, fans, TV): about **15 hours**
- 500W load (add fridge and decoder): about **9 hours**
- 1,200W load (add a 1.5HP inverter AC): about **3.5 to 4 hours**

## Signs your battery is dying

- The system switches off much earlier at night than it used to
- The inverter shows full charge but drops fast once the sun goes down
- Lead-acid batteries are swollen, leaking or getting very hot
- You are topping up distilled water more often (tubular batteries)

## Should you replace with lithium?

In almost every case we see in Rivers, Delta and Bayelsa, yes. A lithium upgrade typically gives you more usable storage in a smaller space, no maintenance and a battery that outlasts several lead-acid replacements. Whether you can keep your current inverter depends on its charging profile and voltage, and we check that on site.
