Before you buy solar, know your load. It takes 15 minutes with a pen and paper.

## Step 1: List your appliances

Walk round the house. Write down everything you want on solar.

## Step 2: Find the watts

Look at the label on each item. If it shows amps only:

```
Watts ≈ Amps × 220
```

## Step 3: Write the hours

How many hours a day is it on?

## Step 4: Multiply

```
Watts × Hours = Watt-hours (Wh) per day
```

## Example: 2-bedroom flat in Rumuola

<div className="table-scroll">

| Appliance | Watts | Qty | Hours/day | Wh/day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LED bulbs | 10 | 8 | 6 | 480 |
| Fans | 60 | 3 | 10 | 1,800 |
| TV + decoder | 100 | 1 | 6 | 600 |
| Fridge | 150 | 1 | 8 (cycling) | 1,200 |
| Phones + router | 30 | 1 | 12 | 360 |
| **Total** | | | | **4,440Wh** |

</div>

So this home uses about **4.4kWh a day**.

## Step 5: Peak load

What might be on **at the same time**? Lights + fans + TV + fridge = about 550W. Add ironing (1,000W) = about 1,550W. A 3kVA inverter covers that.

## What the numbers tell you

- **Daily energy** → panels and battery size
- **Peak load** → inverter size
- **Night energy** (6pm to 7am) → battery size
