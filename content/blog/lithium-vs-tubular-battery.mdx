If you live in Port Harcourt, Warri or Yenagoa, you have seen tubular batteries. They are the big tall ones. Many inverter shops sell them. Now lithium batteries are everywhere too. Which one should you buy?

## The short answer

For **solar**, buy lithium. For a **small inverter** that only covers short NEPA outages, tubular can still work.

## Why tubular dies fast on solar

Solar batteries work every single night. That is hard work.

A tubular battery is happy when it stays full and only gives a little power. When you drain it every night, its plates wear out. If it does not get fully charged the next day, it gets **sulphation**. That is a hard white layer on the plates. It kills the battery.

In the rainy season in Rivers and Bayelsa, many days are cloudy. So tubular batteries often stay half-full. That makes them die even faster.

## Why lithium lasts

Lithium does not mind being half-full. It does not mind daily use. Its BMS (small brain inside) protects it. That is why it can do thousands of cycles.

## Simple cost example

Say you need about 4kWh of power each night.

- **Tubular:** you need about 8kWh of tubular, because you can only use half. You will likely replace it every 2 years.
- **Lithium:** a 5kWh lithium battery gives you that 4kWh and lasts many years.

Over 10 years, tubular means buying again and again. Lithium means buying once.

## When tubular can make sense

- You get **long, steady grid supply** (8+ hours a day).
- You only need power for short outages.
- Your load is small: lights, fans, TV.
- Your budget is very tight **today**.

Even then, read [inverter only vs full solar](/blog/inverter-only-vs-full-solar-system) first.

## Care tips if you already have tubular

1. Top up with **distilled water** only. Never tap water.
2. Keep the battery room cool and airy.
3. Do not drain below half.
4. Clean white powder off the terminals.
