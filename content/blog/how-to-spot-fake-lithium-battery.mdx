Lithium batteries are expensive. That makes them a target for fakes. Here are eight signs to watch for.

## 1. The maths does not add up

**Volts × amp-hours = watt-hours.**

- 51.2V × 100Ah = 5,120Wh (about 5kWh)
- 51.2V × 200Ah = 10,240Wh (about 10kWh)

If a label says "48V 100Ah 10kWh", something is wrong.

## 2. It is too light

LiFePO4 cells are heavy. A 5kWh battery is usually about 40 to 50kg. If it feels like a box of air, be careful.

## 3. No datasheet

Every real battery has a datasheet. It lists capacity, cycles, BMS current and certifications.

## 4. No cell maker

Good batteries say who made the cells, such as CATL or EVE. If the seller says "Grade A" but cannot name the maker, be careful. Read [Grade A vs Grade B cells](/blog/grade-a-vs-grade-b-lithium-cells).

## 5. Price far below others

If one battery costs much less than all others of the same size, ask why. Common answers: used cells, smaller real capacity or weak BMS.

## 6. No BMS details

Ask for the BMS current rating. It should match your inverter. See [what a BMS is](/blog/what-is-a-bms-lithium-battery).

## 7. Vague warranty

"It has warranty" is not enough. Ask how many years or cycles, in writing, with a real address.

## 8. Wrong chemistry

Make sure it says **LiFePO4**. Read [LiFePO4 vs NMC](/blog/lifepo4-vs-nmc-lithium-battery).
