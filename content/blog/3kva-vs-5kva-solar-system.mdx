The two most common home systems in Rivers, Delta and Bayelsa are **3kVA** and **5kVA**. Which one is right for you? It comes down to one question: **do you want to run an AC?**

## Quick compare

<div className="table-scroll">

| | 3kVA | 5kVA |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | 1 to 2 bedrooms | 2 to 3 bedrooms |
| Lights, fans, TV | Yes | Yes |

| Washing machine | Careful | Yes |
| Pressing iron | Only with little else on | Yes |

| Usual panels | 4 × 600W | 5 to 6 × 600W |

## Choose 3kVA if…

- You live in a flat or small bungalow.
- You want the generator off for lights, fans, TV and fridge.
- You do not plan to run an AC on solar.
- Your budget is tight.

A 3kVA with 3.2kWh lithium runs a 400W evening load for about 7 hours.

## Choose 5kVA if…

- You want to iron, wash clothes or use a microwave without switching things off.
- You want one AC for a few hours.
- You run a shop with a freezer.
- You think you will grow your load.

## The AC rule

A 1.5HP AC needs a big burst of power to start. A 3kVA inverter often trips. So if you want AC, go 5kVA or more. If you want AC **all night**, look at 6kVA with 8kWh. Read [can solar power an AC](/blog/solar-ac).

## Do not forget the battery

The inverter decides **what** you can run. The battery decides **how long**. A 5kVA inverter with a tiny battery will still go off early at night. See [how long a solar battery lasts at night](/blog/how-long-will-my-solar-battery-last-at-night).

## Local tip

Many families in [Obio-Akpor](/solar-installation/obio-akpor) and [Effurun](/solar-installation/effurun) start with 3kVA, then add an AC a year later and need a new inverter. If an AC is in your plans, start at 5kVA.
