Many lithium batteries say **"Grade A cells"** on the box. What does it mean? And can you trust it?

## What a cell grade is

Battery cells come off a factory line. Each one is tested. They check:

- How much power it holds
- How well it charges and drains
- How close it is to the other cells

Cells that pass everything are **Grade A**. Cells that miss some targets are sold as **Grade B** or lower.

## Side by side

<div className="table-scroll">

| | Grade A | Grade B |
|---|---|---|
| Passed full tests | Yes | No, or partly |
| Capacity | As rated | May be lower |
| Cell-to-cell match | Close | Can vary |
| Life | Longest | Often shorter |
| Price | Higher | Lower |

## Why matching matters

A battery is a team of cells. If one cell is weak, the whole battery stops when that cell is empty. A team of matched Grade A cells works together for years.

## The "Grade A" sticker problem

Anyone can print "Grade A". To be sure:

1. Ask **who made the cells**.
2. Ask for the **cell model**.
3. Ask for a **test report** if possible.
4. Get a **written warranty** that covers capacity.
