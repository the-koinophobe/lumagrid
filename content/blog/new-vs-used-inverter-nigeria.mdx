Used inverters are sold in markets and online across Nigeria. Some are good. Many are not. Here is how to think about it.

## Risks of a used inverter

1. **Worn parts.** Fans, capacitors and relays wear out.
2. **Old design.** Many older units have no solar input or no lithium setting.
3. **Modified sine wave.** Some cheap units are not pure sine wave. Read [pure vs modified sine wave](/blog/pure-sine-wave-vs-modified-sine-wave).
4. **No warranty.**
5. **Fake ratings.** A "5kVA" label may not mean 5kVA of real power.

## If you still want used

- Ask for the brand and model. Look up the datasheet.
- Check it is **pure sine wave**.
- Check for **lithium** battery settings.
- Listen to the fan. Smell for burning.
- Test it with a real load, like a fridge.

## The battery problem

A cheap inverter with wrong charge settings can ruin an expensive lithium battery. The battery is the costliest part. Do not risk it to save a little on the inverter. See [inverter prices in Nigeria](/blog/inverter-price-nigeria).
