Kitchens use short bursts of heavy power. Here is how that works on solar.

## How much power

<div className="table-scroll">

| Appliance | Watts | Typical use | Energy used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microwave | 1,000–1,500W | 5 minutes | About 0.1kWh |
| Electric kettle | 1,500–2,200W | 3 minutes | About 0.1kWh |
| Blender | 300–1,000W | 2 minutes | Very little |
| Air fryer | 1,200–1,800W | 15 minutes | About 0.4kWh |
| Hot plate / cooker | 1,500–3,000W+ | 1 hour+ | 2kWh+ |

</div>

## The rule: one at a time

These items use a lot of power **at once**. Your inverter has a limit. Use one heavy item at a time. Read [inverter overload: what to do](/blog/inverter-overload-what-to-do).

## Which inverter

- **3kVA:** microwave or blender with light loads on. Kettle maybe.
- **5kVA:** microwave, kettle, blender more freely.

## What about cooking?

Electric cookers and hot plates run for a long time. On batteries, that is expensive. Most solar homes keep **gas** for cooking.
