If you live in Warri, Effurun, Asaba, Sapele or Ughelli, your light comes from **BEDC**. Some streets get good hours. Many do not. And when light comes, the voltage can be too low to run a fridge or AC.

## The two BEDC problems

### 1. Outages

Light goes for hours or days. You turn on the generator.

### 2. Low or unstable voltage

Light is "on" but fans crawl, bulbs are dim and the fridge will not start. Bad voltage can damage motors and electronics.

## How solar helps

<div className="table-scroll">

| Problem | What solar + hybrid inverter does |
|---|---|
| Outage | Runs your home from battery and panels |
| Low voltage | Ignores bad grid power; supplies steady 220–230V |
| High bills | Uses solar first, buys fewer units |
| Generator fuel | Cuts it to almost zero |

</div>

## Why hybrid is right for most of Delta

A hybrid inverter can use BEDC power when it is good. It can top up your battery from the grid. That means a smaller battery is enough. When the grid is bad, it switches away.

In [Asaba](/solar-installation/asaba), many streets get more grid hours than Warri. Hybrid makes the most of that.

## Off-grid for bad areas

Some streets and new estates get almost no BEDC supply. There, a bigger battery and more panels make sense. Read [solar for new estates without a transformer](/blog/solar-for-new-estates-without-transformer).

## Protect your appliances now

Even before solar, do this:

1. Use a stabiliser for fridges and ACs on BEDC power.
2. Unplug sensitive items during storms.
3. Do not run heavy loads on very low voltage.
