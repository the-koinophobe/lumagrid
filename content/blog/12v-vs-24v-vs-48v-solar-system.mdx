Every solar battery system runs at a set voltage. The common ones are **12V, 24V and 48V**. The right one depends on how big your system is.

## The simple rule

<div className="table-scroll">

| System size | Best voltage | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| Up to about 1kVA | 12V | A room: lights, fan, phone |
| About 1.5kVA to 3kVA | 24V | Small flat, no AC |
| 5kVA and above | 48V | Family home, AC, pump, office |

</div>

## Why higher voltage is better for bigger systems

Power = voltage × current.

To deliver 3,000W:

- At **12V**, you need about **250 amps**.
- At **24V**, about **125 amps**.
- At **48V**, about **63 amps**.

Big current needs very thick cables. It also makes heat, and heat wastes power. At 48V, the current is low. Cables are thinner and cooler. Less power is lost.

## Why 48V is now normal

- Most 5kVA, 6kVA and 10kVA hybrid inverters are 48V.
- Most home lithium batteries are 48V (labelled 51.2V).
- 48V is easy to grow. You can add another battery later.

Our 6kVA family system in [Port Harcourt](/solar-installation/port-harcourt) and [Asaba](/solar-installation/asaba) runs at 48V.

## When 24V is fine

A small 3kVA system for a one or two-bedroom flat can work at 24V. If you might add an AC later, start at 48V instead. Read [3kVA vs 5kVA](/blog/3kva-vs-5kva-solar-system).

## A safety note

Do not mix batteries of different voltages. Do not connect a 24V battery to a 48V inverter. Always follow the inverter's voltage.
