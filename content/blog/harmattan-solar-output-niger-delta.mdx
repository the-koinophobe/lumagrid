From about **December to February**, harmattan haze can reach **Port Harcourt, Warri, Asaba and Yenagoa**. It is milder than in Kano or Abuja, but you will notice it on your solar output.

## What changes

<div className="table-scroll">

| Effect | Why |
|---|---|
| Hazy skies | Dust in the air blocks some sunlight |
| Dusty panels | No rain to wash them |
| Cooler mornings | Panels actually work a bit better when cool |

</div>

## What to do

1. **Rinse panels** every 2 to 4 weeks. See [how to clean panels safely](/blog/how-to-clean-solar-panels-safely).
2. **Watch your inverter's daily solar total.** If it drops, clean.
3. **Use the grid or generator top-up** on very hazy days if you have a hybrid inverter.

## Harmattan + soot

In Port Harcourt, dust mixes with soot. That makes a sticky film. Read [soot and solar panels](/blog/soot-port-harcourt-solar-panels).

## Good news

Dry season also means dry roofs. It is a great time to install. Read [best time to install solar](/blog/best-time-to-install-solar-port-harcourt).
