Between your solar panels and your battery, there is a box that controls charging. It is called a **charge controller**. There are two kinds: **MPPT** and **PWM**.

## What a charge controller does

Panels make power at one voltage. Batteries need it at another. The controller fixes the gap and stops the battery from overcharging.

## PWM: the simple one

PWM connects the panel to the battery in quick on-off pulses. It is cheap. But it pulls the panel voltage down to the battery voltage. Any extra voltage is **wasted**.

## MPPT: the smart one

MPPT keeps checking for the panel's best point. That is the "maximum power point". It then converts the extra voltage into extra current. Less is wasted.

## Why it matters in the rainy season

In Port Harcourt and Yenagoa, many rainy-season days are cloudy. Every bit of power counts. MPPT squeezes more charge from weak light. That means fuller batteries at night.

## Do you need a separate controller?

Most hybrid inverters we install have MPPT **built in**. You do not buy it separately. Some old inverters have no solar input. Then you need an external MPPT controller, or a new inverter. Read [inverter only vs full solar](/blog/inverter-only-vs-full-solar-system).

## Check the limits

Every MPPT has limits: maximum voltage and maximum power. Wiring too many panels can damage it. That is why panel count is a design job. See [how many panels for a 5kVA inverter](/blog/how-many-solar-panels-for-5kva-inverter).
