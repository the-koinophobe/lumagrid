Roofs in Rivers, Delta and Bayelsa come in many types. Long-span aluminium in new estates. Old corrugated zinc in many family houses. Stone-coated tiles in newer duplexes. Concrete decks on some storey buildings.

Solar can go on all of them. But the installer must do different things.

## Roof by roof

| Roof | Solar? | What we check |
|---|---|---|
| Long-span aluminium | Yes, easy | Purlin spacing, sheet thickness |
| Corrugated zinc (new) | Yes | Timber strength, fixing points |
| Old rusty zinc | Fix first | Rust, holes, weak wood |

## The golden rule: fix to the structure

Panels catch wind. Rainy-season storms are strong. The rails must bolt into the **rafters or purlins** under the sheet. Screwing only into the sheet is not safe.

## Sealing

Every bolt makes a hole. Every hole must be sealed with the right washers and sealant. Good sealing means no leaks.

## Old roofs

Some older houses in **Sapele**, **Old GRA Port Harcourt** and **Agbor** have tired roofs. We check them during the free site visit. If the roof is weak, we tell you what to fix first, or suggest a [ground mount](/blog/roof-mount-vs-ground-mount-solar).

## Coastal areas

On **Bonny** and **Brass**, salt air rusts cheap metal. Use aluminium rails and stainless bolts. Read [solar in salty coastal air](/blog/salt-air-solar-bonny-brass-coastal).
