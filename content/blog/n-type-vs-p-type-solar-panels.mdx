Solar panels keep getting better. One big change is the move from **P-type** to **N-type** cells. You will see these words on datasheets. Here is what they mean.

## The simple idea

Inside every solar cell, silicon is mixed with a tiny bit of another material. This is called **doping**.

- **P-type** uses boron. It was the normal type for many years.
- **N-type** uses phosphorus. It is newer.

You do not need to remember that. Just remember the results.

## Side by side

<div className="table-scroll">

| | N-type | P-type |
|---|---|---|
| Age of technology | Newer | Older |
| Power lost each year | Lower | Higher |
| Early power drop (first months) | Very small | Larger |
| Heat loss | A little lower | A little higher |
| Price | A little higher | Lower |
| Common names | TOPCon, HJT | PERC |

</div>

## Why it matters in our heat

Roofs in Port Harcourt, Warri and Yenagoa get very hot at midday. All panels lose a bit of power when hot. N-type loses a little less. That means more power on hot afternoons.

## Why it matters over 25 years

A panel slowly makes less power as it ages. Good panels lose around half a percent per year. N-type panels usually lose a bit less. After 20 years, that can be several percent more power.

## How to tell

Look at the datasheet. Look for words like:

- **N-type** or **TOPCon** or **HJT** = N-type
- **PERC** = usually P-type

Brands like Jinko and Longi sell both. Ask for the exact model name.

## Should you pay more for N-type?

If the price gap is small, yes. If your budget is tight, a good P-type mono panel from a trusted brand is still fine. What matters more is that the panel is genuine. Read [how to spot fake panels](/blog/fake-substandard-solar-panels-nigeria).
