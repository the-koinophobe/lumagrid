The battery decides how long your power lasts at night. The two most common sizes we install are **5kWh** and **10kWh**.

## Usable power

Lithium batteries let you use most of their power, but not all. Plan on about 85 to 90%.

<div className="table-scroll">

| Battery | Usable (about) |
|---|---|
| 5kWh | 4 to 4.5kWh |
| 10kWh | 8 to 9kWh |

</div>

## How long each lasts

<div className="table-scroll">

| Night load | 5kWh battery | 10kWh battery |
|---|---|---|
| 300W (lights, fans, TV) | About 14 hours | About 28 hours |
| 500W (add fridge, Wi-Fi) | About 8 to 9 hours | About 16 to 18 hours |
| 1,200W (add 1.5HP inverter AC) | About 3.5 hours | About 7 hours |

</div>

## Who needs 5kWh

- Flats and bungalows with no AC at night
- Shops that close at night but keep a freezer

## Who needs 10kWh

- Homes that run an AC at night
- Homes with a freezer **and** lots of evening use
- People who want backup on cloudy rainy-season days

## The cost

Lithium storage costs roughly ₦220,000 per kWh in our installed systems. So going from 5kWh to 10kWh adds about **₦1.1M**. That is why we size the battery carefully. Read [lithium battery price in Nigeria](/blog/lithium-battery-price-nigeria).

## Tip: buy for the night, not the day

In the day, panels run your home. The battery is only for night and cloudy hours. List what you use **after 6pm**. That tells you your battery size. Use the [calculator](/calculator).
